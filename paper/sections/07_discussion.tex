\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
% =============================================================

The pipeline introduced in this paper --- point-source field
$\to$ activating function on Jaxley's $G$ $\to$ equivalent
compartmental current $\to$ Jaxley integration --- runs through a
single JAX trace and returns gradients for every parameter that enters
it. The verification section establishes that the operator and the
convergence behaviour are correct; the empirical section establishes
that the simulator is fast enough to sweep the design space without
surrogate approximation. Together they enable gradient-based search
over waveform shape, the design problem this extension was built to
support.

\paragraph{Cross-validation against NEURON.}
The three-step cross-check of \cref{sec:res-neuron} pairs analytical
correctness on a uniform cable (\cref{sec:results-verify}) with
external parity on a branched BBP Pyr cell. Single-compartment
kinetics agree to $\pm 1$ spike and $\pm 0.04\,$mV AP peak across the
BBP Pyr and PV channel sets; full-morphology Pyr matches NEURON to
within one integration step on spike timing and $\sim\!0.3\,$mV in
subthreshold RMSE; under extracellular stimulation, the same Pyr cell
matches NEURON at four physical sites to a median per-site RMSE of
$0.446\,$mV. The principal residuals are characterised: a
$-0.38\,$mV resting-potential offset traced to our Euclidean-distance
approximation of BBP's path-distance apical $I_h$ gradient
(\cref{sec:cells-bbp}), and a compartmentalisation difference (Jaxley
$700$ compartments vs.\ NEURON $1\,131$ segments under its
\texttt{lambda}-rule discretisation) that surfaces as $\sim\!50\,\mu$s
first-spike timing drift and a $+0.49\,$mV suprathreshold AP-peak drift.
The PV (LBC) cell does not run stably in Jaxley's default integrator
at full morphology; this is a real limit on our cell-model coverage
and we discuss it in \cref{sec:limitations}.

\paragraph{Comparison with gradient-free pipelines.}
Genetic search over waveforms
\cite{grill2015model,wongsarnpigoon2010energy} returns no gradient
direction for a candidate that is close to but short of an activation
target; surrogate networks trained on NEURON outputs
\cite{melissano2024efficient} introduce an approximation error that
must be re-incurred whenever the underlying biophysical model
changes. A single backward pass through \texttt{jx.integrate} returns
$\partial \ell / \partial w(t)$ at every input timestep for any
differentiable scalar objective $\ell$ and avoids both costs. As a
sharper receipt than the position gradient of \cref{sec:res-grad}, an
Adam loop on $\mathrm{MSE}(\hat{v}, v_\mathrm{target})$ on the full
BBP Pyr morphology recovers \texttt{gNaTs2T} from a $-15\,\%$
perturbation to within $+0.13\,\%$ in nine steps
(\texttt{fit\_demo}). The point of this fit is end-to-end gradient
flow on a realistic cell, not optimiser performance. A selectivity Pareto sweep against a non-target cell, or a
charge-minimising waveform under a spike-elicitation constraint,
follows the same template.
