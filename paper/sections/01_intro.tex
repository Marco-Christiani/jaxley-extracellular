\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
% =============================================================

Clinical neurostimulation devices, including deep brain stimulators and
spinal cord stimulators, deliver pulsed extracellular currents through
electrodes placed near the target tissue. Therapeutic outcome depends on a
small set of waveform parameters --- pulse width, shape, amplitude,
frequency, polarity --- whose effect on neural activation is nonlinear and
sensitive to local geometry. In present clinical practice these parameters
are tuned by hand, guided by symptom observation across patient visits.
Computational design has been explored as an alternative, but the
standard pipeline pairs a volume-conductor field model with
NEURON~\cite{Hines2001NeuronAT} and is bound by NEURON's CPU-serial
integration when sweeping the design space.

Studies searching for energy-efficient or selective waveforms in this
pipeline have either used genetic
algorithms~\cite{grill2015model,wongsarnpigoon2010energy} or trained
surrogate neural networks on NEURON
outputs~\cite{melissano2024efficient}. Genetic search returns no
gradient and requires many forward simulations per design. Surrogates
are fast at inference but introduce approximation error and have to be
retrained whenever the underlying biophysical model changes.

Jaxley~\cite{deistler2025jaxley} is a JAX-native compartmental
simulator. It supports automatic differentiation, JIT compilation,
\texttt{vmap}, and the modern \texttt{jax.sharding} API for
multi-device execution. It does not currently expose extracellular
stimulation. Our extension constructs the discrete activating function
from Jaxley's own axial diffusion operator and injects the result as a
compartmental current through the public stimulus API. Because the pipeline routes through this public API
rather than the solver, \texttt{jit}, \texttt{vmap}, and \texttt{grad}
continue to compose with it, and gradients of voltage observables flow
end-to-end with respect to electrode position, waveform parameters,
and tissue conductivity. This places direct waveform optimisation on
the same compute budget that gradient-free pipelines already spend on
forward simulations.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A differentiable extracellular-stimulation pipeline through
  Jaxley's public stimulus API, with no fork of the upstream simulator.
  \item Numerical verification of the resulting operator: agreement
  with Jaxley's internal voltage vector field to $\sim\!10^{-13}$
  relative error in float64, $O(\Delta x^2)$ convergence to the
  analytical activating function, and end-to-end gradient flow through
  \texttt{jx.integrate}.
  \item Three-step cross-validation against NEURON --- single-compartment
  kinetics, full-morphology Pyr intracellular, and full-morphology Pyr
  under extracellular stimulation --- with characterised residuals at
  each step.
  \item TPU-scale empirical results on the Blue Brain Project (BBP)
  L2/3 pyramidal cell: a strength--duration sweep across distance and
  polarity, a charge-balanced biphasic grid of $315$ valid forward
  simulations, and a throughput--scaling curve at $\sim\!34\times$
  wall-clock speedup over serial single-core NEURON at $B = 1000$.
\end{enumerate}
